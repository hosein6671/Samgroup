# SAM Group Website Voice and Messaging

Status: English master, copy-review draft. This document defines how the public website should sound. It does not approve technical, commercial, corporate, or legal facts.

## Positioning

SAM Group presents a broad petroleum and lubricant portfolio to professional buyers who need to identify a suitable product, review its available information, and open a precise commercial or technical conversation.

The website should make a complex catalogue easier to navigate. It should never compensate for missing evidence with superlatives, vague claims, or invented numbers.

## Primary message

**Petroleum and lubricant products, organised around the way professional buyers specify, source, and supply them.**

## Supporting messages

1. **Start with the requirement.** Buyers can browse by product family, application, industry, or grade name.
2. **Review before you enquire.** Product pages prioritise usable descriptions, specifications, and available technical documents.
3. **Bring us the difficult brief.** When a standard catalogue product is not the right fit, the custom-solutions route captures the application and required specification.
4. **Turn interest into a workable request.** Quote, sample, and technical enquiries ask for the details needed to move the conversation forward.

## Voice

| Trait                | We do                                                               | We do not                                                                   |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Technically literate | Use recognised product and supply terminology accurately.           | Add jargon to sound authoritative.                                          |
| Direct               | Say what the buyer can inspect or do next.                          | Use ceremonial corporate language.                                          |
| Commercially useful  | Ask for grade, quantity, packaging, and destination where relevant. | Hide the next step behind “Learn more.”                                     |
| Evidence-led         | Qualify typical values and link claims to documents.                | Publish unsupported capacity, market, certification, or performance claims. |
| International        | Write clear global English that translates cleanly.                 | Use idioms, slang, or culture-specific wordplay.                            |

## Tone by context

- Product discovery: concise, structured, factual.
- Technical content: precise, qualified, calm.
- Sales conversion: confident and specific, never pressuring.
- Forms and errors: practical, respectful, solution-oriented.
- Quality: transparent about evidence and document availability.
- Legal: supplied and approved by qualified legal counsel.

## Preferred terminology

- Use **product family**, **grade**, **application**, **technical data**, **packaging**, **destination**, **Incoterm**, and **request a quote**.
- Use **typical values** only when values are explicitly non-contractual and sourced.
- Use **available on request** only when the operational team has confirmed availability.
- Prefer **suitable for** or **intended for** over unqualified performance promises.
- Prefer **Request a quote**, **Request a sample**, **View products**, and **Talk to technical sales** over generic CTAs.

## Prohibited until verified

Do not publish unverified figures or statements about capacity, years in business, countries served, customers, distributors, facilities, laboratories, delivery performance, response time, MOQ, lead time, payment terms, certificates, approvals, compliance, test capability, or named customer results.

Do not use: “world-class”, “market-leading”, “best”, “premium quality”, “unmatched”, “guaranteed performance”, “global leader”, or “state-of-the-art” unless a specific, reviewable basis is supplied.

## CTA hierarchy

1. Request a quote — buyer has a defined commercial requirement.
2. Request a sample — buyer needs product evaluation.
3. Talk to technical sales — requirement needs interpretation.
4. View products — buyer is still discovering the range.
5. Read the technical data — buyer is validating a specific product.

## Translation rule

English is the semantic master. Persian and Arabic should preserve meaning, qualification, and CTA intent rather than imitate English sentence structure. Product names, grades, standards, units, and Incoterms remain technically exact.
